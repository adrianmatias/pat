# expand-contract

**Problem.** Strategy work rots in one mode: endless options with no decision (expansion forever),
or premature commitment to the first plausible path (contraction too early).

**Shape.** The **alternating loop** — work breathes in two phases that must not contaminate each
other, each logged separately (one log per phase, so expansion cannot read the contraction's
verdicts and start self-censoring):

1. **breath-in (expand)** — generate paths toward the objective, **wide and unjudged**. No
   criticism lives here. Numbered options survive until contractively killed.
2. **breath-out (contract)** — audit every thread against the objective's scenarios. The northstar
   test: *does this shorten the path to a scenario, or is it movement entertaining itself?* Kill
   with reasons (nothing dies silently), then **contract exactly ONE next concrete move — and
   execute it.**

Alternate each round. Separate sessions or personas may own the phases when the harness supports
it. Rounds are numbered and handed off (`continue: round N`).

**Rules.**
- Phase purity is the pattern: critique inside breath-in kills the divergent half; divergence
  inside breath-out kills the decision. Log them apart.
- Exactly one move contracts per round. "One move" is the unit of progress — a contracted list of
  five things is expansion wearing a suit.
- Kills are recorded with reasons; unrecorded kills are how the same bad idea returns.
- A cycle with no kill and no move is a stall — that is data: the objective may be unclear (drop to
  the steering ladder).

**When.** Roadmaps, uncertain terrain, "what should we even build", goal-driven continuations where
direction drifts.

**When not.** Known changes (`sandwich`), decomposed batches (`cheap-swarm`), single answers
(`duel`). Do not expand-contract a task you can already specify.

**Cost profile.** Cheap breadth (fan-out is `base`) + one judge pass per round; the expensive part
is the single contracted move, executed for real.
